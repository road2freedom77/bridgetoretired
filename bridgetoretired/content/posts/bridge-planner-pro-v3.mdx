We shipped Pro v3 today. Here is what changed, why each change matters, and what it means for your retirement plan.

## What Was Wrong With v1 and v2

The original Pro planner had three structural problems that made it less useful than it should have been:

**Social Security income was not modeled.** The POST-59½ sheet projected withdrawals through life expectancy but never subtracted SS income — so the portfolio looked like it had to fund 100% of spending forever, even after SS started. That made every plan look worse than it actually was.

**The Roth Ladder suggested nothing.** The conversion suggestion was $0 in every bridge year because the formula was broken. The ACA cliff cross-check existed in concept but never fired.

**There was no Monte Carlo.** The planner showed one deterministic path at a fixed return rate. That looks clean but misses the most important risk in early retirement: what happens if the bad years come first.

Pro v3 fixes all three — and adds several things that weren't there at all.

## What's New in Pro v3

### Monte Carlo Simulator — the most important addition

The MONTE CARLO sheet runs 200 retirement simulations using randomized annual returns drawn from a normal distribution centered on your INPUTS return assumption, with volatility set by your portfolio standard deviation.

Each simulation asks one question: does the money last to your life expectancy? The results block shows:

- **Success rate** — what percentage of 200 simulations end with a positive balance
- **Median final balance** — the middle outcome
- **10th percentile** — the bad luck scenario
- **90th percentile** — the good luck scenario

Press F9 to re-run with a new set of 200 random sequences.

**Why this matters:** A plan that shows "funded to age 90" in the deterministic POST-59½ sheet might show a 40% Monte Carlo success rate. That gap is sequence of returns risk — the risk that a crash in years 1-3 of retirement permanently damages the portfolio before it can recover. The deterministic projection assumes returns arrive in a smooth, predictable order. They never do.

**Benchmarks:** 90%+ is robust. 75-90% is acceptable with spending flexibility. Below 75% means the plan needs structural changes before retiring.

### 9-Sheet Planning System

Pro v3 has 9 sheets, all updating automatically from a single INPUTS tab:

**INPUTS** — one entry point for everything. Retirement age, account balances, spending, filing status, state, SS benefit, volatility. Change any blue cell and all 9 sheets recalculate.

**BRIDGE** — year-by-year withdrawal plan from retirement to 59½. Taxable and cash first, then Roth, then 401k. Rows auto-blank past the bridge period. Works for bridge lengths from 0 to 14.5 years.

**TAX ESTIMATE** — federal tax estimate for every bridge year. Switches between MFJ and Single brackets from INPUTS. Shows gross income, standard deduction, taxable income, estimated federal tax, and state tax per year.

**ROTH LADDER** — suggested Roth conversion amounts per bridge year, calculated as the smaller of remaining 12% bracket space and ACA cliff headroom. The ACA cliff cross-check flags each year automatically. Overwrite any year to convert more — the cliff warning updates instantly.

**POST-59½** — full projection from 59½ to life expectancy. Social Security income now correctly reduces annual withdrawals from your claiming age. The planner shows the exact year SS starts and how much it reduces portfolio draws.

**MONTE CARLO** — 200-scenario simulator as described above.

**RISK FLAGS** — 9 automated checks with color-coded conditional formatting. Green for passing, orange for warnings, red for hard stops. Updates in real time as INPUTS change.

**REBALANCE** — annual allocation tracker. Enter target percentages by asset class. Current values link to INPUTS balances. Drift alerts and buy/sell signals update automatically.

**COVER** — sheet guide with color coding reference and disclaimer.

### ACA Cliff Cross-Check in the Roth Ladder

The ROTH LADDER sheet now has a live ACA cliff check in column G. For each bridge year, it calculates your projected MAGI — 401k withdrawals plus the suggested Roth conversion — and compares it against the estimated 400% FPL threshold for a 2-person household ($81,760 for 2026).

If the conversion keeps you under the cliff: green check.
If the conversion pushes you over: amber warning with a note to trim the conversion amount.

The suggested conversion defaults to the smaller of bracket space and cliff headroom — so the default is already ACA-safe. But if you overwrite the suggestion to convert more, the warning fires.

This matters because a Roth conversion that saves $5,000 in future taxes can cost $15,000 in lost ACA subsidies in the same year. The cross-check catches that before you file.

### Social Security Modeled in POST-59½

The POST-59½ sheet now correctly subtracts inflated SS income from annual portfolio withdrawals once you reach your claiming age. The SS benefit grows with inflation from the first projection year.

In practical terms: if you enter a $24,000 SS benefit at claiming age 67, the planner reduces annual portfolio draws by the inflation-adjusted equivalent of $24,000 starting in the year you turn 67. The RISK FLAGS sheet shows the exact dollar impact of delaying SS from your current claiming age to 70.

### Color-Coded RISK FLAGS

All 9 RISK FLAGS now use Excel conditional formatting that updates automatically:

- Green — check passes
- Orange — review needed (high withdrawal rate, bridge gap, thin cash buffer, ACA cliff exposure)
- Red — hard stop (penalty risk, portfolio depletes before life expectancy)
- Gold — advisory (SS delay opportunity)

The colors react to the actual formula output in real time. Change INPUTS and the flag colors update instantly.
